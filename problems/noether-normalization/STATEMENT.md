## Why not classical / why formalize-only

Settled commutative algebra: if `A` is a finitely generated
algebra over a field `k`, then there exist
algebraically independent `y₁, …, y_d ∈ A` such that `A`
is module-finite (equivalently integral, once finite-type
is given) over the polynomial subalgebra `k[y₁, …, y_d]`.
The integer `d` is the Krull dimension / transcendence
degree. Completely classical (Emmy Noether 1926).

## Pinned convention (exact)

**v1 is the existence of an algebraically independent
family over which `A` is module-finite**, for
`Algebra.FiniteType k A` with `k` a field.
Prefer `Module.Finite` as the conclusion (already
equivalent to integral once finite-type holds).

Suggested pin:

```text
-- Level A (not labelled Noether): the polynomial ring
-- itself is module-finite over itself (d = n generators,
-- identity map). Glue: already-upstream
-- Algebra.IsIntegral.finite. Not labelled Noether.

-- Level B namesake
theorem noether_normalization
    (k A : Type*) [Field k] [CommRing A] [Algebra k A]
    [Algebra.FiniteType k A] :
    ∃ (d : ℕ) (y : Fin d → A),
      (AlgebraicIndependent k y) ∧
      Module.Finite (MvPolynomial (Fin d) k) A
```

The `MvPolynomial (Fin d) k →ₐ[k] A` is `aeval y`.
`Algebra.FiniteType k A` is load-bearing.
`Field k` is load-bearing (the classical linear/Noether
change of variables uses a field; finite fields need the
`x_i ↦ x_i - x_n^{q^i}` form — pin *infinite `k` first*
if budget bites, with a comment residual for finite `k`).
Algebraic independence is load-bearing (`d` is the
transcendence degree).

**Level A may land only** the identity case
`MvPolynomial σ k` is finite over itself, plus a one-step
integral substitution for a single algebraic relation,
**not** labelled Noether. Use
`Algebra.IsIntegral.finite` — **do not re-prove
integral + finite-type ⇒ module-finite**.

**Level B** is the namesake: induct on the number of
generators; after a change of variables, the last
generator is integral over the first `n-1`. Do not sorry
the namesake; honest partial is allowed (comment residual,
not `sorry`). Infinite-`k` linear change is an acceptable
v1 cap; finite-`k` power substitution is residual.

## Proof sketch (classical, Atiyah–Macdonald)

Level A: `aeval id : MvPolynomial σ k →ₐ[k] MvPolynomial σ k`
is the identity, so the polynomial ring is module-finite
over itself; already-upstream `IsIntegral.finite` converts
an integral finite-type extension into `Module.Finite`.
**Not** labelled Noether.

Level B: write `A = k[x₁,…,x_n]`. If the `x_i` are
algebraically independent, done. Otherwise a nonzero
relation `F(x₁,…,x_n) = 0`; after a change of variables
(`x_i = y_i + y_n^{m^i}` over infinite `k`, or the
Noether power trick over finite `k`), `y_n` is integral
over `k[y₁,…,y_{n-1}]`, and induction applies. Cap two
levels. No Nullstellensatz. No going-up namesake.
