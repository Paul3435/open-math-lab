# Algebra Skill Pack

## Purpose

Attack strategies for problems involving groups, rings, fields, vector spaces, modules, and algebraic structures.

## When to Use This Pack

Use algebra lens when the problem involves:

- **Group theory**: subgroups, homomorphisms, cosets, quotient groups, group actions, Sylow theorems
- **Ring theory**: ideals, quotient rings, polynomial rings, unique factorization domains, principal ideal domains
- **Field theory**: field extensions, Galois theory, finite fields, algebraic vs. transcendental elements
- **Linear algebra**: vector spaces, bases, linear transformations, eigenvalues, determinants, matrix decompositions
- **Module theory**: free modules, torsion, exact sequences
- **Lattice theory**: posets, lattices, Boolean algebras
- **Universal algebra**: varieties, free structures, term algebras

## Core Strategies

### 1. Homomorphism & Isomorphism Theorems
- First Isomorphism Theorem: G/ker(φ) ≅ im(φ)
- Second & Third Isomorphism Theorems for lattice of subgroups
- Check if map is well-defined, injective, surjective, preserves operation
- Use to reduce structure to simpler quotient

### 2. Lagrange's Theorem & Cosets
- |G| = |H| · [G:H] for subgroup H of finite group G
- Order of element divides order of group
- Sylow theorems for p-subgroups
- Orbit-stabilizer theorem for group actions

### 3. Ideal Structure & Quotient Rings
- Maximal ideals ⟺ quotient is a field
- Prime ideals ⟺ quotient is an integral domain
- Chinese Remainder Theorem for coprime ideals
- Principal ideal domains have unique factorization

### 4. Polynomial Ring Techniques
- Division algorithm in F[x] for field F
- Irreducibility tests: Eisenstein criterion, mod p reduction, rational root theorem
- Unique factorization in UFDs (Z[x], F[x])
- Minimal polynomials for algebraic elements

### 5. Linear Algebra Reduction
- Gaussian elimination for solving systems
- Rank-nullity theorem: dim(V) = rank(T) + nullity(T)
- Eigenvalue/eigenvector computation
- Jordan normal form or diagonalization
- Determinant properties (multilinear, alternating)

### 6. Field Extensions & Galois Theory
- Degree formula: [K:F] = [K:E][E:F]
- Algebraic closure and splitting fields
- Galois correspondence: subgroups ↔ intermediate fields
- Fundamental theorem of Galois theory
- Finite fields: F_q has q = p^n elements, Galois group ≅ Z/nZ

### 7. Structure Theorems
- Fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups
- Structure theorem for finitely generated modules over PIDs
- Cayley's theorem: every group embeds in a symmetric group
- Artin-Wedderburn theorem for semisimple rings

### 8. Computational Algebra
- Gröbner bases for polynomial ideal membership
- Smith normal form for integer matrices
- Factorization algorithms in Z[x], F_p[x]
- Discrete logarithm and index calculus (finite fields)

## Common Pitfalls & Dead Ends

### Anti-Patterns: Do NOT Do This

1. **Assuming commutativity without justification**
   - **Why it fails**: Most groups are non-abelian; ring multiplication often non-commutative.
   - **Example**: Matrix multiplication AB ≠ BA in general.
   - **Fix**: Explicitly state when commutativity is assumed or proved; use bracket notation [a,b] = aba⁻¹b⁻¹ for commutators.

2. **Confusing "generates" with "freely generates"**
   - **Why it fails**: <g> = G means g generates G, not that elements are freely generated (no relations).
   - **Example**: Z/nZ is generated by 1, but n·1 = 0 (relation exists).
   - **Fix**: Distinguish cyclic groups (one generator with relations) from free groups (generators with no relations).

3. **Applying Lagrange's theorem backwards**
   - **Why it fails**: |H| divides |G| does NOT imply H exists as a subgroup (unless p-groups or abelian).
   - **Example**: A₄ (alternating group on 4 elements) has order 12 but no subgroup of order 6.
   - **Fix**: Use Sylow theorems for existence of p-subgroups; construct subgroup explicitly.

4. **Ignoring zero divisors in rings**
   - **Why it fails**: ab = 0 does NOT imply a = 0 or b = 0 unless R is an integral domain.
   - **Example**: In Z/6Z, [2]·[3] = [0] but neither [2] nor [3] is [0].
   - **Fix**: Check if ring is an integral domain before canceling; use prime ideals for domain property.

5. **Assuming polynomial roots in base field**
   - **Why it fails**: Polynomial may be irreducible over F but factor over extension K.
   - **Example**: x² + 1 is irreducible over R but factors as (x - i)(x + i) over C.
   - **Fix**: Specify field explicitly; use splitting field or algebraic closure when roots needed.

6. **Misapplying rank-nullity theorem**
   - **Why it fails**: Only applies to linear transformations between finite-dimensional vector spaces.
   - **Example**: Does not hold for infinite-dimensional spaces (e.g., shift operator on ℓ²).
   - **Fix**: Verify finite dimensionality; use spectral theory for infinite-dimensional cases.

7. **Confusing ideals and subrings**
   - **Why it fails**: Every ideal is a subring, but not every subring is an ideal.
   - **Example**: Z ⊆ Q is a subring but not an ideal (not closed under Q-multiplication).
   - **Fix**: Check closure under ring multiplication, not just subring multiplication.

8. **Over-relying on computational algebra tools**
   - **Why it fails**: Gröbner basis computation can be infeasible (doubly exponential in worst case).
   - **Example**: Computing Gröbner basis for large ideal with many variables may not terminate.
   - **Fix**: Use heuristic simplifications first; escalate to symbolic tools only for small instances.

### Known Hard Subdomains (Budget Carefully)

- **Galois theory for large extensions**: Computing Galois groups of high-degree polynomials is hard.
- **Representation theory**: Character tables, induced representations require specialist knowledge.
- **Non-commutative algebra**: Free algebras, Hopf algebras, quantum groups are advanced.
- **Computational algebra**: Gröbner bases, polynomial factorization can be exponential-time.

## Budget & Stopping Criteria

- **Token budget per reduction attempt**: 100k tokens max
- **Compute budget**: Gröbner basis or factorization beyond degree 10 polynomials needs justification.
- **Time cap**: 30 minutes per attack session before checkpoint.

### Stop When

- Group/ring structure is fully determined → hand to Formalist for Lean encoding
- Counterexample found (e.g., element order doesn't divide group order) → document and hand to Reviewer
- No progress after 3 strategy pivots → write failed-closed summary
- Reduction to known algebra theorem (e.g., Sylow, isomorphism theorems) → verify with Reviewer

## Handoff Criteria

### → Adversarial Reviewer

Hand off when:

- You have an algebraic "proof" (homomorphism, isomorphism, ideal structure)
- Computational evidence (e.g., computed subgroup lattice for |G| ≤ 100)
- Reduction to known theorem (verify reduction is valid)
- Claim about group/ring structure (needs counterexample search)

**Deliverable to Reviewer:**
- `ATTACK_LOG.md` with strategy attempts
- Proof sketch with explicit homomorphisms or structure maps
- Computational data (subgroup lattice, character table, etc.)
- Explicit claim and hypotheses
- Remaining gaps (e.g., "homomorphism well-definedness not verified for quotient")

### → Formalist

Hand off when:

- Proof is clean and Reviewer-approved
- Statement involves standard algebraic structures (groups, rings, fields, vector spaces)
- Dependencies on Mathlib.Algebra identified
- Edge cases (trivial group, zero ring, etc.) documented

**Deliverable to Formalist:**
- Structured proof outline with lemma dependencies
- Explicit structure definitions (group operation, ring axioms, etc.)
- References to Mathlib lemmas (e.g., `QuotientGroup.mk_surjective`, `Ideal.IsPrime.mem_or_mem`)
